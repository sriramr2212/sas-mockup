A Working Glossary

The language of the loom.

Every craft has its own vocabulary. This is ours — a working glossary of the weaves, motifs, regions and care rituals that shape a South Indian handloom saree.

Weaves & Techniques

Kanjivaram (Kanchipuram silk)
The heirloom silk saree of Tamil Nadu, woven in Kanchipuram from pure mulberry silk and real gold-silver zari, with the body, border and pallu often woven separately and interlocked (see korvai).
Silk cotton
A saree with a pure mulberry silk warp and a mercerised cotton weft — softer and cooler than pure silk, but with the sheen and drape of silk. A signature Sri Aishwarya weave.
Handloom
A saree woven on a pit-loom or frame-loom operated entirely by human hands and feet. Carries the government Handloom Mark and shows small, alive variations no machine can replicate.
Powerloom
A saree woven on an electricity-driven mechanical loom — faster and cheaper, but stiffer, more uniform, and cannot carry the Handloom Mark.
Korvai
The traditional Kanchipuram technique of weaving the border and body in different colours on separate shuttles and interlocking them at the selvedge. A true korvai border can be gently pulled away from the body without unravelling.
Petni (Pitni)
The technique of joining a contrast pallu to the body of a Kanjivaram — you can often see a fine zigzag line where the two colours meet.
Mubbagam
A three-shuttle Kanjivaram woven with three different colours — body, border and pallu each in a distinct shade. One of the most technically demanding weaves.
Jacquard
A loom attachment that lets the weaver produce complex figured patterns automatically. Handloom jacquard is still hand-thrown; powerloom jacquard is mechanised.
Dobby
A simpler loom attachment used for small geometric patterns — checks, stripes, small buttis.
Tussar
A wild silk from the Antheraea moth — coarser, more textured, and naturally beige-gold. Prized for summer sarees.
Semi-silk / polycotton
A saree that blends silk or cotton with polyester or viscose. Everyday-wearable and easy to care for, but not a pure-yarn saree.

Yarn & Zari

Mulberry silk
The finest cultivated silk, spun by Bombyx mori silkworms fed on mulberry leaves. The standard silk used in a genuine Kanjivaram.
Pure zari
A silver thread electroplated with real gold, twisted around a silk or cotton core. Tarnishes slowly and beautifully, and is the only zari that qualifies a saree as an heirloom.
Half-fine zari
Copper with a thin gold-coloured coating. Cheaper, but darkens within a few years. Common in imitation Kanjivarams.
Tested zari
Zari certified by the Silk Mark Organisation of India — the mark you should look for on any premium Kanjivaram.
Denier
A unit for the fineness of a silk thread. Lower denier = finer yarn, softer drape.
Mercerised cotton
Cotton treated with caustic soda to give it strength, sheen and better dye uptake. The cotton weft in a fine silk cotton saree is almost always mercerised.

Motifs & Patterns

Rudraksham
A repeating bead-like motif inspired by the rudraksha seed, sacred to Lord Shiva. Common on traditional Kanjivaram borders.
Mayilkann (peacock eye)
A small motif shaped like the eye of a peacock feather, worked into borders and buttis.
Kuyilkann (koel eye)
A finer, more delicate version of mayilkann, often used in silk cotton sarees.
Annam
The mythical swan motif — a symbol of grace and discernment, woven into pallus and borders.
Yaali
A composite mythical creature (part lion, part elephant, part horse) carved on temple pillars and woven onto grand bridal Kanjivarams.
Temple border
A saw-tooth border shaped like the gopuram of a South Indian temple — the most recognisable of all Kanjivaram borders.
Butti / buta
A small repeating motif scattered across the body of the saree.

Parts of a Saree

Pallu / mundhi
The decorated end of the saree that drapes over the shoulder — usually the most ornate section.
Mundani
The Tamil term for the pallu, especially in wedding contexts.
Body
The main length of the saree between the two borders — usually the most understated section, in a single field colour.
Border
The woven strip along both long edges of the saree. On a korvai Kanjivaram, the border is a different colour from the body.
Contrast pallu
A pallu in a contrasting colour to the body — a classic Kanjivaram signature.

Regions & Drapes

Kanchipuram
The temple town near Chennai that gives Kanjivaram silk its name — home to the community of weavers who have made these sarees for over 400 years.
Arni
A weaving town in Tamil Nadu known for lighter, everyday silk sarees at a friendlier price point.
Thirubuvanam
A weaving cluster near Kumbakonam known for finely woven pure silks and silk cottons.
Madisar (9-yard)
The traditional 9-yard drape of Tamil Brahmin women, worn for weddings, seemantham and religious rituals.
Koorai
The bridal saree gifted to the bride by the groom's family in a Tamil wedding — traditionally a nine-yard madisar in Kanchipuram silk.
Dhoti / veshti
The unstitched lower garment worn by South Indian men, often in matching handloom cotton or silk.

Care & Storage

First wash
The very first wash of a new silk saree. Always dry-clean — home washing can bleed dye and shrink the yarn.
Refolding
The practice of unfolding and refolding a stored saree every 3–4 months along a different crease, so the zari never wears through the same line.
Muslin wrap
A soft, breathable cotton cloth used to wrap folded silk sarees for storage. Never use plastic — it traps humidity and stains the zari.
Silk Mark
The government-issued label certifying that a saree is made of 100% natural silk. Look for it on the corner of any silk saree you buy.
Handloom Mark
The government-issued label certifying that a saree was woven on a handloom. The one mark powerloom sarees cannot legally carry.
